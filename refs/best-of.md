# Best of the four references

**Sources:**
- **Measurements:** `refs/<name>/` holds `cuts.txt` (scene > 0.15), `palette.json` and `beats.md` for each video.
- **Frames and contact sheets:** these stay local (gitignored).
- **Timestamps:** taken from the change curve or from 10 fps strips; `~` means read by eye.
- **What this file covers:** one technique per video, the strongest one, with the timestamps that show it. Each technique is named as a category: pacing, type, transitions, camera, sound or structure.

**Sound sync was measured, not heard.**
- Only `launch` shows cutting that is demonstrably locked to the music (its colour cards).
- In the other three, cuts sit 50–210 ms from the nearest audio onset, so sync isn't shown.
- Sound is therefore nobody's strongest technique here.

## launch: **Transitions.** The camera flies through the UI instead of cutting

**What it is:** 12 beats in 15.1 s, but only **one** hard cut (the end card at 13.133). Every other beat change is a camera move that passes *through* an element of the outgoing beat into the next one.

**Evidence** (`refs/launch/beats.md`):
- 1.85: blur-zoom. The folder scales up and blurs while the next headline scales down into place.
- 3.60–3.77: an accelerating push-in on the prompt bar. The change per frame climbs from 10 to 47, and the camera flies straight through into the next card with no cut spike.
- 5.40–5.52: the editor slides up over the card, with a slight 3D tilt.
- 7.25–7.40: a push-in and tilt from the full editor down onto the timeline.
- 9.15: a pan to the inspector.
- ~10.0–10.3: a pull-back to the whole editor.
- 11.28 → 13.13: an iris-wipe run accelerates to one wipe every 0.45–0.47 s, on the 129 BPM beat.
- The 0.15 scene filter misses the moves at 1.85, 3.77 and 10.25. That's how smooth they are.

**Don't take:**
- the folder mascot with eyes
- the product's chips, copy and slogan
- the wordmark
- the four-colour card sequence
- the beat order: hook → prompt → brief → editor → timeline → inspector → pills → colour cards → end card
- the opening composition, a centred subject on a blush→lavender gradient. That is the banned "centred title on gradient".

## launch2: **Type.** The caret is the camera

**What it is:** a two-scale typographic system.
- **Small, centred sentences** are typed word by word: these are statements.
- **Giant headlines** are typed a key at a time and overflow the frame. The frame **pans to keep the caret in view**, so you read the line as it's written.
- The product icon arrives as the last "character".

**Evidence** (`refs/launch2/beats.md`):
- 2.83–2.97: a small typed line jumps to giant scale.
- 3.20, 3.53, 3.90, 4.27, 4.60: single-frame keystroke spikes, every 0.33–0.37 s.
- 4.5–5.0: the line overflows and the frame pans with the caret.
- ~4.9–5.3: the icon is typed in before the name.
- 25.3–28.9: reprised faster, about one key per 0.1 s, with only the tail of the line visible.
- Small statements: 6.5, 23.0 (with a text-selection highlight at 24.5), 29.0 and 48.0.

**Don't take:**
- the app name, icon and every line of copy
- the macOS desktop, menu-bar popover and wallpaper
- the fictional "Northwind" data
- the people in the video-call tiles
- the use-case montage objects (invoice, parcel label, departure board, sticky note)
- the 3D card explosion at 50.9–54.4, which is a generic particle burst under the house rules
- the order: collage → typed intro → desktop demo → montage → typed headline → indexed feature tour → explosion → end card

## launch3: **Camera.** The device holds its place while the world changes, and every product shot moves

**What it is:** the product (a device) is the anchor.
- Cuts change the world *behind* it while the device keeps roughly its position and angle, so the eye never has to search.
- Every product shot carries one continuous camera move with shallow depth of field.
- The closing move reveals that the scene was inside the product's own editor.

**Evidence** (`refs/launch3/beats.md`):
- 8.13 → 9.3 → 10.5: the same phone, at the same angle, over ocean, then flowers, then a branch (a hard cut, then two short whips).
- 13.77–15.60: a dolly-in on a laptop, whose lid closes at 15.5–16.2.
- 16.4–17.0: a foldable unfolds.
- 24.93–26.17: a dolly left across a monitor.
- 29.8–31.7: a phone rotates up through a blurred wordmark.
- 35.5–39.0: a pull-back shows the laptop is inside the editor UI, then the camera pushes to the timeline (37.0) and the inspector (38.0–38.8).
- Product shots sit on a steady ~3 s grid: 8.13, 11.03, 13.30, 16.30, 19.30, 21.93, 24.93.

**Don't take:**
- the wordmark and icon
- the "orbit" demo app and the people in it
- all the photography (flower fields, ocean, desert, moss, branch) and the inflated-blue end-card material
- the named hardware (phone, tablet and laptop models)
- the scrambling **pixel/dot-matrix typeface**, and the all-caps headline at 32.5–34.0. Both are banned by the house style: monospace-style readouts and condensed all-caps display.
- the 2 → 28 device counter as content
- the order: typed line → flat screens → 3D reveal → device worlds → counter → devices → headline → editor reveal → end card

## launch4: **Structure.** One fictional story runs through every feature

**What it is:** a single invented project goes through *every* feature in the film. It's a product launch with one fixed ship date and the same small team throughout. So the features read as **one workflow**, not a list, and the viewer always knows what is being worked on. The typed caret bookends it.

**Evidence** (`refs/launch4/beats.md`):
- 12.0: a chat asks about the launch decision.
- 13.0: meeting notes record the date moving.
- 13.5: a search across connected apps finds it.
- 14.0–15.8: a typed request turns last week's meeting into a plan.
- 18.87–20.10: the plan streams in with the same owners and dates.
- 20.6–22.0: it becomes a board.
- 23.5–27.4: agents are added to that board.
- 45.0–53.0: the final question is about the same launch.
- 55.3–57.0: a closing callback line.
- Caret motif: 0.0 (opening caret), 14.0, 33.5, 36.0, 40.0 and 49.5 (a giant close-up of the prompt).

**Don't take:**
- the logo, wordmark, version number and release copy
- the AI face icon
- the specific launch scenario, its names and its dates
- third-party model and app brands (in the model picker and the connector icons)
- the condensed all-caps title at 28.23 (banned)
- the order: caret → logo → workspace claim → AI → plan → board → agents → skills → custom agents → home prompt → phone → system zoom-out → logo

## Ranking for Safari OS

Safari OS is a back office for safari tour operators. Its modules form a pipeline: enquiry → quote and costing → itinerary → client portal → dispatch and compliance. The current film (`films/safarios`, 1920×1080, 20 s, 120 BPM) gives each module a 2 s beat.

| Rank | Strength | Fit | Why | Cost in this studio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **launch4 structure:** one story through every feature | **High** | Safari OS *is* a pipeline, so following one booking from enquiry to dispatch is the product story. The current film breaks continuity: the hook's enquiry says **March** (`film.js:129`), but the client portal shows **14–18 Nov** (`film.js:326`), and the board puts four different bookings side by side (`film.js:211–214`). Use one fictional booking (one lead name, one tour, one date, one guest count) from hook to dispatch. | **Low:** a data change in `film.js`, no new motion |
| 2 | **launch transitions:** the camera flies through UI | **High** | Each Safari OS beat is a UI card that can open into the next: the board card → the costing sheet → the itinerary header → the portal → the driver row. The `CAMERA` / `HEAVY` springs and the pure `seek(t)` handle continuous pushes cleanly. | **Medium:** per-beat camera keys. These moves are invisible to scene-cut detection, so cue sync must use `window.cueBox`, as it already does. |
| 3 | **launch2 type:** the caret is the camera | **Medium** | It fits the AI ask bar (cue `askbar`) and a typed hook ("one enquiry…"). On a 1080 × 1920 phone cut, a giant overflowing line shows only a few letters at a time. Keep the words short, and keep it to the hook or a single headline. | **Low:** text plus a pan track; keystroke SFX are easy to synthesise |
| 4 | **launch3 camera:** device-anchored worlds | **Low** | Safari OS is a desktop web app for operators, not a device product. The technique depends on photography of places. For safari, that means wildlife and landscape photos, which need licences: under the brief rules that's a `BLOCKED.md` item, not a guess. Its type style is banned here. The transferable part is the anchored match cut on **flat brand grounds** (forest green, clay) instead of photos. | **High:** licensed imagery plus 3D device builds |

**Recommendation:** combine 1 and 2. Follow one booking through the modules, with a camera that moves *through* each card into the next. Use 3 only for the hook and the ask bar. Leave 4 out.
